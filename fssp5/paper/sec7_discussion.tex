\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The conditions of this paper are of two kinds. The barriers of
\cref{sec:right,sec:left} are proved for every number of states and concern the
half-line alone. The conditions of \cref{sec:early} concern the first rows after
the reflection and the right ends, and we used them through satisfiability
solvers, for four and five states.

The conditions of \cref{sec:early} do not exclude every rule. For the germ of
$\delta_{14}^{\mathrm d}$, which has complexity $15$, conditions (G1)--(G3) with
$T=10$ and all $n\ge12$ have a model, and so do (G1)--(G3) with $T=7$ and all
$n\ge9$ together with (G5) for $9\le n\le20$. In the second model, the lines of
lengths $9$, $11,\dots,17$ and $19$ have both end cells, but not all cells, in
state $\Fq$ at time $2n-2$, and no cell in state $\Fq$ before
(\cref{fig:model}(a); both models are in the supplementary material). This suggests that excluding such rules needs
conditions on the middle cells of the firing row, which are expensive to
encode: for $n=15$, the dependency cone of the middle cell of the firing row
contains $76$ of the $120$ cells of $R_{15}$ (\cref{fig:model}(b)).

The computational part has three limitations. Its results concern four and
five states and, apart from the reduction of \cref{cor:reduce}, finitely many
lengths. The formula generators are trusted programs: they are tested in
\cref{sec:method} and cross-checked by a second, independent encoder, but they
are not formally verified, and neither is the proof checker
\texttt{lrat-check}. And the behaviour of particular
rules beyond the simulated ranges, such as the patterns of the half-lines of
$\delta_{12}$ and $\delta_{14}$, is not known.

We close with four questions.
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
\item Does a five-state minimal-time solution exist?
\item Is there a condition on the middle of the reflected triangles that is as
economical as \cref{lem:G}?
\item Can the bound $(k-1)^{2s}\ell$ of \cref{lem:left} be made polynomial in
$s$?
\item Do Balzer's conditions exclude six states? Do they exclude five states
without (B2) at the borders (\cref{sec:balzer})?
\end{enumerate}
