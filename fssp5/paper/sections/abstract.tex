Whether the firing squad synchronization problem has a minimal-time
solution with five states has been open since Mazoyer's six-state solution
of 1987. We develop tools for minimal-time solutions with any number of
states and measure how far they reach on this question. All lines are
described by one half-line diagram and one reflected triangle per length,
and every triangle is a function of two anti-diagonals of the half-line.
On the half-line we prove two necessary conditions: a pumping lemma, which
forces its non-periodic part to reach the line $i=t/3$, and a left-border
lemma, which forbids periodic behaviour in any wedge at the border; in
particular the half-line of a minimal-time solution is never eventually
regular. A propositional encoding, validated against Mazoyer's solution,
gives an LRAT-certified proof, checked in six seconds, that no four-state
minimal-time solution exists. For five states we exhibit rules that
synchronize every line of length at most $14$ in minimal time, and we show
with certified solver calls that every five-state minimal-time solution
uses at least $15$ distinct neighbourhoods on its half-line below the
anti-diagonal $t+i=78$. The half-lines of low complexity that survive all
our tests are irregular, and search-size estimates of about $27$ nodes for four states against $10^{11}$
to $10^{19}$ for five quantify why the question remains open.
