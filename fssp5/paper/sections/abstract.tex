Whether the firing squad synchronization problem admits a minimal-time
solution with five states has been open since Mazoyer's six-state solution
of 1987. We develop general tools for minimal-time solutions and measure how
far they reach on this question. (i)~All lines are encoded by one half-line
diagram and one reflected triangle per length, and every triangle is a
function of two anti-diagonals of the half-line. (ii)~We prove two
necessary conditions on the half-line alone, valid for any number of
states: a pumping lemma, which forces the non-periodic part of the
half-line to reach the line $i=t/3$ (a speed-$1/3$ barrier), and a
left-border lemma, which bounds the periodic stretches of the anti-diagonals
near the left border and hence forbids periodic behaviour in any wedge at
the border. (iii)~A propositional encoding of the bounded problem,
validated against Mazoyer's solution extracted from its Coq formalization,
gives an LRAT-certified proof, checked in six seconds, that no four-state
minimal-time solution exists, even without the left quiescence condition.
(iv)~For five states we exhibit explicit rules that satisfy the
half-line conditions up to length $40$ and synchronize every line of length
at most $13$, and, with a chaotic half-line, every line of length at most
$14$, in minimal time; the left-border lemma shows that the half-line of the
first two can never be completed to a solution. Combining both lemmas with certified
solver calls, we show that every five-state minimal-time solution uses at
least $15$ distinct neighbourhoods on its half-line below the anti-diagonal
$t+i=78$. Random-probe estimates of the natural look-ahead search tree give
about $27$ nodes for four states but $10^{11}$ to $10^{19}$ for five, which
quantifies why the question remains open.
