Whether the firing squad synchronization problem admits a minimal-time
solution with five states has been open since Mazoyer's six-state solution
of 1987. We develop general tools for minimal-time solutions and measure how
far they reach on this question. (i)~All lines are encoded by one half-line
diagram and one reflected triangle per length, and every triangle is a
function of two anti-diagonals of the half-line. (ii)~We prove a pumping
lemma: in every minimal-time solution, with any number of states, the
inputs of the reflected triangles of two lengths $n<n'$ differ on an
explicit cone of about $n/2$ cells. The lemma constrains the half-line alone
and yields a speed-$1/3$ barrier: the non-periodic part of the half-line
always reaches the line $i=t/3$, which explains the speed-$1/3$ signal of the
classical constructions. (iii)~A propositional encoding of the bounded
problem, validated against Mazoyer's solution extracted from its Coq
formalization, gives an LRAT-certified proof, checked in six seconds, that
no four-state minimal-time solution exists, even without the left
quiescence condition. (iv)~For five states we exhibit an explicit rule that
synchronizes every line of length at most $12$ in minimal time, whose
half-line obeys the pumping constraints up to length $40$ and never fires up
to anti-diagonal $78$, and whose last configuration before firing is
uniform. Hence any refutation of five-state solutions must involve lines of
length at least $13$. Random-probe estimates of the natural look-ahead
search tree give about $27$ nodes for four states but $10^{11}$ to $10^{19}$
for five, which quantifies why the question remains open.
