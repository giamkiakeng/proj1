\section{Reflected triangles and a pumping lemma}\label{sec:pumping}

\subsection{Relative coordinates}

Fix $n\ge2$. For a cell $(t,i)$ of the line of length $n$ put
$\tau:=t-(n-1)$ (time elapsed since the front reached the right end) and
$\kappa:=n-i$ (distance to the right end). Then
\[
R_n=\{(\tau,\kappa):\ 0\le\kappa\le\tau\le n-1\},
\]
the right border sits at $\kappa=-1$ and the left border at $\kappa=n$.
A cell $(\tau,\kappa)\in R_n$ is computed from
$(\tau-1,\kappa+1),(\tau-1,\kappa),(\tau-1,\kappa-1)$; each of these is
either in $R_n$, or a border, or satisfies $\tau'-\kappa'\in\{-1,-2\}$,
i.e.\ lies on one of the anti-diagonals $t+i=2n-2$, $t+i=2n-3$, where
$C_n$ coincides with $C_\infty$ by \cref{lem:agree}. We call
\[
J_n(\kappa):=\bigl(C_\infty(n-3+\kappa,\,n-\kappa),\;C_\infty(n-2+\kappa,\,n-\kappa)\bigr),
\qquad 0\le\kappa\le n-1,
\]
the \emph{input word} of $R_n$; its two components are the \emph{lower}
($t+i=2n-3$) and \emph{upper} ($t+i=2n-2$) input cells. (For $n=2$ and
$\kappa=0$ the lower cell would lie at time $-1$; it is a predecessor of no
cell of $R_n$, and we set it to $\mathsf L$.)

\begin{lemma}[determinacy]\label{lem:determinacy}
There is a single partial map $\Psi$, depending on $\delta$ only, such that
for every $n\ge2$ and every $(\tau,\kappa)\in R_n$,
$C_n\bigl(n-1+\tau,\,n-\kappa\bigr)$ is obtained by evaluating $\Psi$ at
$(\tau,\kappa)$ on the input word $J_n$ with borders at $\kappa=-1$ and
$\kappa=n$. In particular the value only depends on the input cells and
borders in the backward dependency cone of $(\tau,\kappa)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The recurrence in relative coordinates does not mention $n$; only the
position of the left border does.
\end{proof}

For $(\tau,\kappa)\in R_n$ let $\mathrm{cone}_n(\tau,\kappa)$ be the set
of input cells, i.e.\ of pairs consisting of a position $\kappa'$ and one of
the two anti-diagonals, that are reached from
$(\tau,\kappa)$ by repeatedly passing to the three predecessors, stopping at
input cells and borders; say that the cone is \emph{right-anchored} if it
does not reach the left border $\kappa'=n$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cone}
For $n\ge4$ the cone of the right end at the firing time,
$\mathrm{cone}_n(n-1,0)$, is right-anchored and consists of the upper input
cell at $\kappa'=0$, both input cells at $1\le\kappa'\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$,
and, when $n$ is odd, the lower input cell at $\kappa'=(n+1)/2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A predecessor step changes $\tau$ by $-1$ and $\kappa$ by at most one, and
input cells satisfy $\tau'=\kappa'-1$ or $\kappa'-2$. Starting from
$(n-1,0)$, a path that reaches an input cell at position $\kappa'$ after $s$
steps satisfies $\kappa'\le s$ and $n-1-s=\kappa'-1$ or $\kappa'-2$; hence
$2\kappa'\le n+1$ (upper cells: $2\kappa'\le n$). Conversely, the upper cell
at a position $\kappa'$ with $2\kappa'\le n$ is reached by moving left
$\kappa'$ times and then staying put, and the lower cell at a position
$\kappa'\ge1$ with $2\kappa'\le n+1$ by moving left $\kappa'-1$ times,
staying put down to the cell $(\kappa'-1,\kappa'-1)$ and moving left once
more; all intermediate cells satisfy $\tau\ge\kappa$ and lie in $R_n$. The
lower cell at $\kappa'=0$ is a predecessor of no cell of $R_n$. The largest
position reached is $\lceil n/2\rceil\le n-1$, and a path can reach
$\kappa=n$ only after $n$ left moves, i.e.\ at a negative time, so the left
border is never reached; the right border only contributes the constant
symbol~$\ast$. (The same set
is also computed mechanically in the accompanying code, file
\texttt{pumping.py}.)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[pumping]\label{lem:pumping}
Let $\delta$ be a minimal-time solution and $4\le n<n'$. Then $J_n$ and
$J_{n'}$ differ on $\mathrm{cone}_n(n-1,0)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose they agree there. By \cref{lem:determinacy,lem:cone} the relative
cell $(n-1,0)$ of $R_{n'}$ (which belongs to $R_{n'}$ since $n-1\le n'-1$,
and whose cone is the same set of input positions, far from the left border
$\kappa=n'>n$) has the same value as the relative cell $(n-1,0)$ of $R_n$,
namely $C_n(2n-2,n)=\mathsf F$. Thus $C_{n'}(n'+n-2,n')=\mathsf F$ although
$n'+n-2<2n'-2$, contradicting minimality for $n'$.
\end{proof}

The point of \cref{lem:pumping} is that it constrains the \emph{half-line
diagram only}: no reflected triangle has to be computed. It is the source of
the constraint family $\mathrm{PUMP}(n,n')$ used in \cref{sec:encoding}. As a
sanity check, we verified mechanically that Mazoyer's six-state solution
satisfies $\mathrm{PUMP}(n,n')$ for all $4\le n<n'\le120$.

\subsection{Consequence: a speed-\texorpdfstring{$1/3$}{1/3} barrier}

Write the half-line diagram in the frame of the front:
$\Phi_t(j):=C_\infty(t,\,t+1-j)$ for $0\le j\le t$ (the cell at
\emph{depth}~$j$ behind the front at time~$t$). By the recurrence,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comoving}
\Phi_{t+1}(j)=\delta\bigl(\Phi_t(j),\,\Phi_t(j-1),\,\Phi_t(j-2)\bigr)
\qquad(0\le j\le t),
\end{equation}
with $\Phi_t(-1)=\Phi_t(-2)=\mathsf L$: in this frame the half-line is a
\emph{one-sided} automaton in which information only travels to larger
depths, at most two depths per step. In particular every depth-row
$t\mapsto\Phi_t(j)$ is eventually periodic.
In these coordinates
$J_n(\kappa)=\bigl(\Phi_{n-3+\kappa}(2\kappa-2),\ \Phi_{n-2+\kappa}(2\kappa-1)\bigr)$.

\begin{theorem}[speed-$1/3$ barrier]\label{thm:third}
Let $\delta$ be a minimal-time solution. Every depth-row
$t\mapsto\Phi_t(j)$ is eventually periodic; let $\theta(j)$ be the time from which
it is periodic. Then for every $n\ge4$ there is a depth $j\le n$ with
$\theta(j)>n+\tfrac{j}{2}-\tfrac52$. Consequently
$\limsup_{j\to\infty}\theta(j)/j\ge 3/2$: along infinitely many depth-rows the
transient part extends, up to an arbitrarily small angle, to the line
$i=t/3$ of the original coordinates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Row $0$ satisfies $\Phi_{t+1}(0)=\delta(\Phi_t(0),\mathsf L,\mathsf L)$ and is
eventually periodic; by \eqref{eq:comoving}, row $j$ is a finite-state system
driven by the eventually periodic rows $j-1$, $j-2$, hence eventually
periodic, with some period $P_j$. Fix $n\ge4$ and let $P$ be a common
multiple of $P_0,\dots,P_{n+1}$. If every input cell of
$\mathrm{cone}_n(n-1,0)$ were read in the periodic regime of its row, i.e.\
$n-3+\kappa\ge \theta(2\kappa-2)$ and $n-2+\kappa\ge \theta(2\kappa-1)$ for all cone
positions~$\kappa$, then $J_{n+P}=J_n$ on the cone, contradicting
\cref{lem:pumping}. Cone positions satisfy $\kappa\le\lceil n/2\rceil$, so
the violated inequality concerns a depth $j\in\{2\kappa-2,2\kappa-1\}$ with
$j\le n$ and $\theta(j)>n-3+\kappa\ge n+\frac{j}{2}-\frac52$.
If $\limsup_{j\to\infty}\theta(j)/j<3/2$, there are $\alpha<3/2$ and $\beta$
with $\theta(j)\le\alpha j+\beta$ for all $j$, and enlarging $\alpha$ we may
assume $\alpha\ge\tfrac12$. Then for $n$ large and every $j\le n$ we would
have $(\alpha-\tfrac12)j\le(\alpha-\tfrac12)n<n-\beta-\tfrac52$, i.e.\
$\theta(j)\le n+\tfrac j2-\tfrac52$, a contradiction. Finally a cell of depth
$j$ at time $\theta(j)$ sits at position $\theta(j)+1-j$, which is at least
$\theta(j)/3+1$ when $\theta(j)\ge 3j/2$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{thm:third} explains a priori why every minimal-time construction
contains structure reaching the line $i=t/3$, whose crossing with the
anti-diagonal $t+i=2n-2$ is, up to one cell, the far end of
$\mathrm{cone}_n(n-1,0)$: the speed-$1/3$ signal emitted by the general in
the constructions of Waksman and Balzer \cite{Waksman66,Balzer67} runs along
this line, and in Mazoyer's solution the periodic zone behind the front
is bounded by a line of speed $1/2$; indeed we checked that for $1\le j<700$
the depth-row $j$ is $3$-periodic from time $\theta(j)=2j+2$ when $j$ is even or
$j=1$, and from $\theta(j)=2j+1$ when $j\ge3$ is odd. The theorem also explains the failure of many of
the ``lucky'' partial rules found by the solvers (\cref{sec:five}): their
half-line diagrams are periodic behind the front far beyond the line
$i=t/3$.

\subsection{A second barrier, at the left border}

\Cref{lem:pumping} looks at the right end of the line. The left end gives a
constraint of a different nature, again on the half-line only. Fix $n\ge2$
and number the anti-diagonals of the line of length $n$ from the input
anti-diagonals on: for $c\ge-1$ and $1\le i\le n$ with $c\le i$ put
\[
v_c(i):=C_n(2n-2+c-i,\;i),
\]
the cell of the \emph{chain} $t+i=2n-2+c$ at position $i$. Chains $-1$ and
$0$ are the input anti-diagonals, on which $v_c(i)=C_\infty(2n-2+c-i,i)$ by
\cref{lem:agree}; chains $c\ge1$ lie in $R_n$, and chain $1$ is the return
chain of \cref{lem:return}. The cell $(2n-2+c-i,i)$ has its three
predecessors on the chains $c-2$, $c-1$ and $c$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chains}
v_c(i)=\delta\bigl(v_{c-2}(i-1),\,v_{c-1}(i),\,v_c(i+1)\bigr)\qquad(c\ge1),
\end{equation}
where $v_{c-2}(0)$ and $v_c(n+1)$ stand for the border. If the line of
length $n$ is synchronized at time $2n-2$, then $v_i(i)=\mathsf F$ for
$1\le i\le n$ and $v_c(i)\ne\mathsf F$ for $-1\le c<i$. In the skewed coordinates
$u_c(y):=v_c(y+\lfloor c/2\rfloor)$, \eqref{eq:chains} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slices}
u_c(y)=\delta\bigl(u_{c-2}(y),\,u_{c-1}(y+\varepsilon_c),\,u_c(y+1)\bigr),
\qquad \varepsilon_c=\begin{cases}1,&c\text{ even},\\0,&c\text{ odd},\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $u_0(y)=C_\infty(2n-2-y,\,y)$ and $u_{-1}(y)=C_\infty(2n-2-y,\,y-1)$,
and $u_c(y)$ is the cell $y+\lfloor c/2\rfloor$ at time
$2n-2+\lceil c/2\rceil-y$: it must be $\mathsf F$ if $\lceil c/2\rceil=y$
and must differ from $\mathsf F$ if $\lceil c/2\rceil<y$. Thus each \emph{slice}
$(u_1(y),u_2(y),\dots)$ is obtained from the slice $y+1$ by a sequential
finite-state transducer that reads the components in increasing order and
whose initial state is determined by the two input cells $u_{-1}(y),u_0(y)$: read from right to left, the reflected triangle is a
cascade of finite-state transducers, the left-hand counterpart of the
co-moving description \eqref{eq:comoving} of the half-line.

\begin{lemma}[left border]\label{lem:left}
Let $\delta$ have $k$ states and synchronize the line of length $n$ at time
$2n-2$. Let $s\ge1$, $L\ge1$ and $s+L\le Y\le n-s$, and suppose that the
words
\begin{align*}
&y\mapsto C_\infty(2n-2-y,\,y)\qquad(s\le y\le Y)\qquad\text{and}\\
&y\mapsto C_\infty(2n-3-y,\,y)\qquad(s\le y\le Y-1)
\end{align*}
are $L$-periodic. Then $Y-s\le (k-1)^{2s}L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $s\le y\le Y$ let $\mathbf u(y):=(u_1(y),\dots,u_{2s}(y))$. These are cells
$y+\lfloor c/2\rfloor\le Y+s\le n$ with $\lceil c/2\rceil\le s\le y$, so
they are defined, and for $y>s$ none of them is $\mathsf F$. By
\eqref{eq:slices}, $u_c(y)$ with $1\le c\le 2s$ is computed from components
of index $<c$ of the same slice, from components of index $\le c$ of slice
$y+1$, and from $u_{-1}(y),u_0(y)$; hence there is a map $\Gamma$,
depending on $\delta$ only, with
\[
\mathbf u(y)=\Gamma\bigl(\mathbf u(y+1),u_{-1}(y),u_0(y)\bigr)\qquad(s+1\le y\le Y-1).
\]
Unfolding \eqref{eq:slices} for even $c$,
$u_{2j}(y)=\delta(u_{2j-2}(y),u_{2j-1}(y+1),u_{2j}(y+1))$, shows that
$u_{2s}(y)$ depends only on $u_0(y)$ and $\mathbf u(y+1)$: there is a map $\Gamma'$ with
$u_{2s}(y)=\Gamma'(\mathbf u(y+1),u_0(y))$ whenever $s\le y\le Y-1$. By hypothesis,
$u_0$ is $L$-periodic on $[s,Y]$ and $u_{-1}$ on $[s+1,Y]$.

Suppose $Y-s>(k-1)^{2s}L$. The pairs $(\mathbf u(y),\,y\bmod L)$, $s+1\le y\le Y$,
take at most $(k-1)^{2s}L$ values, so there are $s+1\le y_1<y_2\le Y$ with
$\mathbf u(y_1)=\mathbf u(y_2)$ and $y_1\equiv y_2\pmod L$; put $h:=y_2-y_1$. By downward induction,
$\mathbf u(y)=\mathbf u(y+h)$ for $s+1\le y\le y_1$: this holds for $y=y_1$, and if it holds for
$y+1\le y_1$ then
$\mathbf u(y)=\Gamma(\mathbf u(y+1),u_{-1}(y),u_0(y))=\Gamma(\mathbf u(y+1+h),u_{-1}(y+h),u_0(y+h))=\mathbf u(y+h)$,
because $y+h<y_2\le Y$ and $L\mid h$. In particular $\mathbf u(s+1)=\mathbf u(s+1+h)$, and
since $s+h\le Y-1$ and $u_0(s+h)=u_0(s)$,
\[
u_{2s}(s+h)=\Gamma'(\mathbf u(s+1+h),u_0(s+h))=\Gamma'(\mathbf u(s+1),u_0(s))=u_{2s}(s)=\mathsf F .
\]
But $\lceil 2s/2\rceil=s<s+h$, so $u_{2s}(s+h)$, the cell $2s+h$ at time
$2n-2-h$, is not $\mathsf F$: a contradiction.
\end{proof}

In words: a band of $2s$ chains that crosses a long periodic stretch of the
input anti-diagonals is a finite-state system, so it cannot time the firing
of the cell $2s$, which it must trigger exactly when it arrives there. The
border itself is harmless (cell~$1$ fires because the return chain meets
the border), which is why the lemma starts at $s\ge1$ and pays a factor
exponential in $s$.

\begin{theorem}[left-border barrier]\label{thm:left}
Let $\delta$ be a minimal-time solution. There are no $s\ge1$, $\alpha>0$,
$t_0$ and periods $p,q\ge1$ such that $C_\infty(t+p,i)=C_\infty(t,i)$ and
$C_\infty(t,i+q)=C_\infty(t,i)$ whenever the cells involved lie in the wedge
$\Omega:=\{(t,i):\ s\le i\le\alpha t,\ t\ge t_0\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose the contrary and put $L:=pq$. If $(t,i)$ and $(t-L,i+L)$ lie in
$\Omega$, then so do the intermediate cells $(t-L,i+jq)$ and
$(t-L+jp,i)$, and $C_\infty(t-L,i+L)=C_\infty(t-L,i)=C_\infty(t,i)$. For
the anti-diagonals $t+i=2n-2$ and $t+i=2n-3$ this makes the words of
\cref{lem:left} $L$-periodic on $[s,Y_n]$ for some $Y_n$ with
$Y_n\ge \tfrac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\,2n-\gamma$, where $\gamma$ depends on
$\alpha,L,t_0$ only. For $n$ large, $\min(Y_n,n-s)-s>(k-1)^{2s}L$,
contradicting \cref{lem:left}.
\end{proof}

Say that the half-line of a rule is \emph{eventually regular} if there are
$t_1$, $P\ge1$ and, for each residue $r\in\{0,\dots,P-1\}$, words
$w_0,\dots,w_m$ and nonempty words $z_1,\dots,z_m$ over $W$ and integers
$a_j\ge0$, $D_j\ge0$
such that for all $\nu\ge0$ the configuration $C_\infty(t,1)\cdots
C_\infty(t,t+1)$ at time $t=t_1+r+\nu P$ is the word
\[
w_0\,z_1^{\,a_1+\nu D_1}\,w_1\,z_2^{\,a_2+\nu D_2}\,w_2\cdots z_m^{\,a_m+\nu D_m}\,w_m ,
\]
i.e.\ from some time on the half-line consists of finitely many periodic
regions separated by finitely many signals of rational speeds.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:regular}
The half-line of a minimal-time solution is not eventually regular.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Suppose it is. For each residue $r$, some $D_j$ is positive, because the
length of the configuration grows by $P$ per period; let $j(r)$ be the
least such index. The blocks and junctions to the left of $z_{j(r)}$ have
constant lengths, so the block $z_{j(r)}^{a_{j(r)}+\nu D_{j(r)}}$ starts at a
fixed cell $s_r$ and its length grows linearly in $t$, at least like
$\beta t$ for some $\beta>0$ and $t$ large. Inside it, the state of a cell
depends only on its distance to $s_r$ modulo $|z_{j(r)}|$, and it is the
same at the times $t$ and $t+P$. Put $s:=\max_r s_r$, $q:=\prod_r|z_{j(r)}|$
and $\alpha:=\beta/2$. For $t_0$ large, every cell of the wedge
$\{(t,i): s\le i\le\alpha t,\ t\ge t_0\}$ lies in the block of index $j(r)$
of its residue class, so $C_\infty$ is periodic with periods $P$ in time
and $q$ in space on this wedge, contradicting \cref{thm:left}.
\end{proof}

\Cref{cor:regular} applies whatever the number of states. On a concrete
germ it is used through the following finite test. For words $w_0,\dots,w_m$
and nonempty words $z_1,\dots,z_m$ over $W$ and $\mathbf e\in\mathbb N^m$ put
$X(\mathbf e):=w_0\,z_1^{e_1}\,w_1\cdots z_m^{e_m}\,w_m$, and let
$\mathbf 1_j$ denote the $j$th unit vector.

\begin{lemma}[regularity certificate]\label{lem:certificate}
Let $\delta$ be a rule, $t_1\ge0$ and $P\ge1$. Suppose that the
configuration of the half-line at time $t_1$ is $X(\mathbf e)$ with
$e_j|z_j|\ge2P+|z_j|$ for all $j$, and that the configuration at time
$t_1+P$ is $X(\mathbf e+\mathbf D)$ for some $\mathbf D\in\mathbb N^m$. Then
the configuration at time $t_1+\nu P$ is $X(\mathbf e+\nu\mathbf D)$ for
every $\nu\ge0$, and $C_\infty$ is periodic in time and space on a wedge at
the left border. Consequently no rule that agrees with $\delta$ on the
neighbourhoods occurring in $C_\infty$ up to time $t_1+P$ is a minimal-time
solution.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a word $Y$ over $W$ let $\Lambda(Y)$ be the word formed by the cells
$1,\dots,|Y|+P$ after $P$ steps of the half-line started from $Y$ followed
by quiescent cells, with the border on the left; by \eqref{eq:quiescence}
the cells beyond $|Y|+P$ are still quiescent, and $\Lambda$ maps the
configuration at any time $t$ to the configuration at time $t+P$. We write
$Y[p]$ for the letter at position $p$, with $Y[p]=\mathsf L$ for $p>|Y|$. By
locality, $\Lambda(Y)[p]$ depends only on the letters of $Y$ at the positions
$p-P,\dots,p+P$ (and on the border if $p\le P$). Write $q_j:=|z_j|$. Since
$|\Lambda(Y)|=|Y|+P$, we have $\sum_jD_jq_j=P$.

Let $\mathbf e'\ge\mathbf e$ (componentwise) with
$\Lambda(X(\mathbf e'))=X(\mathbf e'+\mathbf D)$, and fix $j$. Put
$Y:=X(\mathbf e')$, $q:=q_j$, and let block $j$ of $Y$ occupy the positions
$a,\dots,b$, so that $b-a+1=e'_jq\ge2P+q$. The word
$Y':=X(\mathbf e'+\mathbf 1_j)$ satisfies $Y'[p]=Y[p]$ for $p\le b$ and
$Y'[p]=Y[p-q]$ for $p\ge a+q$. By locality, $\Lambda(Y')[p]=\Lambda(Y)[p]$ for
$p\le b-P$ and $\Lambda(Y')[p]=\Lambda(Y)[p-q]$ for $p\ge a+q+P$; since
$a+q+P\le b-P+1$, every position is covered, so $\Lambda(Y')$ arises from
$\Lambda(Y)=X(\mathbf e'+\mathbf D)$ by repeating the factor of length $q$ that
ends at position $b-P$. Block $j$ of $X(\mathbf e'+\mathbf D)$ occupies the
positions from $a+\sum_{i<j}D_iq_i\le a+P$ to $b+\sum_{i\le j}D_iq_i\ge b$,
so this factor, which starts at $b-P-q+1\ge a+P$, lies inside it, and
repeating a factor of length $q$ inside a $q$-periodic block lengthens the
block by $q$. Hence $\Lambda(X(\mathbf e'+\mathbf 1_j))=X(\mathbf e'+\mathbf
1_j+\mathbf D)$. The same comparison, made after each of the $P$ steps,
shows that every neighbourhood used in the computation of $\Lambda(Y')$ is used
in that of $\Lambda(Y)$.

Starting from $\mathbf e'=\mathbf e$ and adding one period at a time, we
obtain $\Lambda(X(\mathbf e'))=X(\mathbf e'+\mathbf D)$ for all
$\mathbf e'\ge\mathbf e$, hence, by induction on $\nu$, that the
configuration at time $t_1+\nu P$ is $X(\mathbf e+\nu\mathbf D)$; all
neighbourhoods involved occur in $C_\infty$ up to time $t_1+P$, so the same
holds for every rule that agrees with $\delta$ on them.

Let $j$ be the least index with $D_j>0$ (it exists since $\sum_jD_jq_j=P$),
put $q:=q_j$, and let $s$ be the first position of block $j$; it does not
depend on $\nu$ because $D_i=0$ for $i<j$. At time $t_1+\nu P$ block $j$
occupies the positions $s,\dots,s+\ell_\nu-1$ with
$\ell_\nu=(e_j+\nu D_j)q\ge\nu$, and the configurations at the times
$t_1+\nu P$ and $t_1+(\nu+1)P$ coincide on the positions
$1,\dots,s+\ell_\nu-1$ and are $q$-periodic on $[s,s+\ell_\nu-1]$. By
locality, for $0\le r<P$ the configurations at the times
$t:=t_1+\nu P+r$ and $t+P$ coincide on $[1,s+\ell_\nu-1-P]$ and are
$q$-periodic on $[s+P,s+\ell_\nu-1-P]$. Since $\ell_\nu\ge(t-t_1)/P-1$,
$C_\infty$ is periodic with periods $P$ in time and $q$ in space on the
wedge $\{(t,i):\ s+P\le i\le t/(2P),\ t\ge t_0\}$ for $t_0$ large, and
\cref{thm:left} gives the last statement.
\end{proof}

The program \texttt{germpersist.py} searches for such decompositions, with
block periods $|z_j|$ up to a bound $q_{\max}$ and blocks of length at
least $2P+2q_{\max}$, at the times $t_1+r$ for every residue $r=0,\dots,P-1$; it
thereby shows that the half-line is eventually regular. It also checks the
induction step directly, by evolving the configurations with one or two
additional periods in each block, and compares the predicted configurations
$100$ periods later with a direct simulation.

\begin{remark}[the right end]\label{rem:right}
The proof of \cref{lem:left} uses neither \cref{lem:agree} nor the position
of the chains inside $R_n$: it only uses the recurrence \eqref{eq:chains}
for $c\ge1$, the firing condition ($v_c(i)=\mathsf F$ if and only if $c=i$)
and the periodicity of the two chains $c=-1,0$. It therefore applies
verbatim to the mirror image of the line, i.e.\ to the rule
$\bar\delta(x_1,x_2,x_3):=\delta(x_3,x_2,x_1)$ and the cells $\bar\imath:=n+1-i$, whose
chains $c$ are the diagonals $t-i=n-3+c$ of the line of length $n$. Hence,
if $\delta$ synchronizes the line of length $n$ at time $2n-2$, the words
\begin{align*}
&y\mapsto C_n(2n-2-y,\,n+1-y)\qquad(s\le y\le Y),\\
&y\mapsto C_n(2n-3-y,\,n+1-y)\qquad(s\le y\le Y-1),
\end{align*}
read along the diagonals $t-i=n-3$ and $t-i=n-4$ from the right end, are
not $L$-periodic when $Y-s>(k-1)^{2s}L$ and $Y\le n-s$. For $y\le n/2$
these cells lie in $R_n$: seen from the right end, the reflected triangles
cannot be periodic over long stretches either. Unlike \cref{lem:left},
this is a constraint on the reflected part of the rule, not on the
half-line. (On Mazoyer's rule, for the lengths $n\le300$ with $n\equiv6
\pmod 7$, $s\le2$ and $L\le6$, these stretches have length at most $7$.)
\end{remark}

\Cref{thm:third,thm:left} complement each other: the half-line of a
minimal-time solution has non-periodic structure reaching the line $i=t/3$
behind the front, and non-periodic structure in every wedge at the left
border. In the constructions of Waksman and Balzer the latter is the family
of signals of speeds $1/(2^j-1)$, which accumulate at the border; in
Mazoyer's solution it is the family of recursive division points
(\cref{fig:hare}). The classical halving idea \cite{Minsky67} sends from
the general a signal of speed $1$, the hare, and one of speed $1/3$, the
tortoise; the hare is reflected at the right border at time $n-1$ and meets
the tortoise in the middle at time $3(n-1)/2$, after which both halves are
treated in the same way, which takes about $3n$ steps in all. The
minimal-time constructions send the signals of all speeds $1/(2^j-1)$ at
once: the reflected signal meets them at the points $n/2$, $n/4,\dots$ at
exactly the times at which the pieces between them can be synchronized
recursively so that all pieces fire at time $2n-2$. The tortoise of speed
$1/3$ runs along the line $i=t/3$ of \cref{thm:third}, and the slower ones
fill every wedge at the border, as \cref{thm:left} requires. We checked
\cref{lem:left} mechanically on Mazoyer's rule for $n\le400$, $s\le3$,
$L\le6$: its periodic stretches near the left border have length at most
$6$, far below the bound. Unlike \cref{lem:pumping}, \cref{lem:left} needs
long periodic stretches (for five states, more than $16L$ cells when $s=1$
and more than $256L$ cells when $s=2$), hence long lines, but it applies to
any half-line that can be computed far enough, which we exploit in
\cref{sec:five}.
