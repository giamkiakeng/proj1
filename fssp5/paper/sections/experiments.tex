\section{Four states: a certified proof}\label{sec:four}

All formulas are produced by the generator \texttt{gencnf.py}; models are
decoded into rule tables and re-simulated by the independent brute-force
checker \texttt{fsspcheck.c}. Solvers: Kissat~4.0.4 \cite{Kissat20} and
CaDiCaL~3.0.1 \cite{CaDiCaL2} (or CaDiCaL~1.9.5 through PySAT
\cite{PySAT18} for incremental runs). Times are single-core wall-clock times
on a cloud virtual CPU.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:four}
No rule with four states $\{\mathsf L,\mathsf G,\mathsf A,\mathsf F\}$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:quiescence} synchronizes all lines of lengths $2,\dots,9$ in
minimal time. In particular there is no four-state minimal-time solution,
even without the condition $\delta(\ast,\mathsf L,\mathsf L)=\mathsf L$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:agree,lem:return} and \eqref{eq:neverfire} every such rule
yields a model of $\mathrm{MT}_4(\{2,\dots,9\};16)$ (for $k=4$ there is a
single auxiliary state and no symmetry breaking). CaDiCaL proves this
formula unsatisfiable in $51$\,s and emits an LRAT proof \cite{CruzFilipeEtAl17} of $177$\,MB
($41$\,MB compressed), which \texttt{lrat-check} of the \textsf{drat-trim}
suite \cite{WetzlerHeuleHunt14} verifies in $5.7$\,s. The SHA-256 digests of
the formula and of the proof are listed in \ref{app:repro}.
\end{proof}

The bound $9$ is sharp for the plain formula: $\mathrm{MT}_4(\{2,\dots,8\};14)$
is satisfiable, and an independent depth-first enumeration (program
\texttt{fsspdfs.c}, which fills the table lazily in the order in which
transitions are needed) finds the numbers of partial rules listed in
\cref{tab:four}, counted on the transitions they use; it reaches zero exactly
at the lengths $2,\dots,9$, in agreement with the solver. With the half-line extended to
anti-diagonal $78$ the lengths $2,\dots,8$ already suffice. The pumping
constraints alone are weak for four states: together with the half-line up
to anti-diagonal~$78$, the lengths $2,\dots,7$ remain satisfiable.

\section{Five states: the frontier}\label{sec:five}

\subsection{Lucky rules}

For five states the table has $94$ free transitions with five values each,
against $43$ with four values for four states (in both cases the neighbourhoods
$(\ast,y,\ast)$ and the two quiescence transitions are excluded): the search
space grows by a factor of about $5^{94}/4^{43}\approx10^{40}$. \Cref{tab:five} lists representative instances.
All of them are satisfiable, and in each case the decoded rule fails for
some length that was not encoded; we call such rules \emph{lucky}. Two kinds
occur. Rules of the first kind have a \emph{periodic} half-line behind the
front (\cref{fig:diagrams}(b)); they can even synchronize an isolated long
line such as $n=20$, but they violate the pumping lemma massively (155 of the
pairs $4\le n<n'\le40$ for the rule shown). Rules of the second kind have a
chaotic half-line which, without the half-line constraints, typically fires
at some point. Once the half-line is extended and
the pumping constraints are imposed, the half-lines produced by the solver
change character: in the
models we examined in detail---\cref{fig:diagrams}(c) and the rules of
\cref{thm:frontier}, whose common half-line consists of a front of $\mathsf G$'s, a
solid wake of $\mathsf G$'s and a region $\mathsf{A}\,\mathsf{L}\,\mathsf{A}\,\mathsf{L}\cdots$
behind a boundary of speed exactly $1/3$---a region delimited by a line of
slope $1/3$ appears, as predicted by \cref{thm:third}. Chaotic half-lines
that never fire satisfy the pumping constraints as well; one of them is
discussed after \cref{cor:germ12}.

\subsection{Five-state rules for all lengths up to fourteen}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:frontier}
The five-state rules $\delta_{12}$ and $\delta_{13}$ of
\cref{tab:delta12,tab:delta13} have the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $\delta_{12}$ synchronizes the line of length $n$ at time $2n-2$ for
  every $2\le n\le 12$, and one step before firing all cells are in state
  $\mathsf A$. It does not synchronize the line of length $13$: a cell fires
  at time $23$.
\item $\delta_{13}$ synchronizes the line of length $n$ at time $2n-2$ for
  every $2\le n\le 13$, and one step before firing all cells are in state
  $\mathsf A$ whenever $4\le n\le 13$. It does not synchronize the line of
  length $14$: a cell fires at time $21$.
\item The half-line diagrams of $\delta_{12}$ and $\delta_{13}$ coincide on
  all cells $(t,i)$ with $t+i\le 78$; these cells use $22$ neighbourhoods
  besides $(\mathsf L,\mathsf L,\mathsf L)$, on which the two rules agree, and
  none of them is a firing cell.
\item Both rules satisfy $\mathrm{PUMP}(n,n')$ for all $4\le n<n'\le 40$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each property is a finite computation on the explicit tables, carried out
by direct simulation: (a), (b) and the failures at $n=13$ and $n=14$ with
the checker \texttt{fsspcheck.c}, (c) with \texttt{check\_halfline.py}
and \texttt{germcompare.py}, and (d) with \texttt{pumping.py}, which recomputes the cones of
\cref{lem:cone} by breadth-first search (\ref{app:repro}).
\end{proof}

The rule $\delta_{12}$ was found by Kissat on
$\mathrm{MT}_5(\{2,\dots,12\};78,40)$ with links and the uniform pre-firing
constraint (\cref{tab:five}); $\delta_{13}$ was found by the same solver
after fixing the $22$ half-line neighbourhoods of $\delta_{12}$ and adding
the length~$13$, in $108$\,s. The two rules differ in $57$ of the remaining
entries. Consequently, the necessary conditions used in this paper,
restricted to lengths at most $13$ (even together with the pumping
constraints up to $40$ and the half-line up to anti-diagonal $78$), are
satisfiable (\cref{prop:chaotic} below extends this to length $14$), and
the requirement ``uniform configuration before firing'', satisfied by
Mazoyer's solution, does not help below length $12$. The half-line shared by the two rules
consists of a front of $\mathsf G$'s, a solid wake of $\mathsf G$'s and,
behind a boundary of speed exactly $1/3$, a region
$\mathsf{A}\,\mathsf{L}\,\mathsf{A}\,\mathsf{L}\cdots$ that is periodic in
time and space. By the left-border barrier this architecture cannot be
completed to a solution:

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:germ12}
No rule that agrees with $\delta_{12}$ on the $22$ neighbourhoods of
\cref{thm:frontier}(c) synchronizes the line of length $518$ in minimal
time. In particular no minimal-time solution has the half-line of
$\delta_{12}$ and $\delta_{13}$ below anti-diagonal~$78$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Simulating the half-line with these $22$ neighbourhoods (and
$(\mathsf L,\mathsf L,\mathsf L)$) only, no other neighbourhood occurs on
the cells with $t+i\le1034$; hence every such rule has the same diagram
$C_\infty$ there. On the anti-diagonal $t+i=1034$ the cells $2,\dots,259$
are all in state $\mathsf A$, and on $t+i=1033$ the cells $2,\dots,258$ are
all in state $\mathsf L$. \Cref{lem:left} with $k=5$, $n=518$, $s=2$,
$L=1$ and $Y=259$ would give $257\le4^4=256$.
\end{proof}

So no choice of the remaining transitions can rescue this architecture.
The lemma only bites at length $518$, whereas the two rules themselves
already fail at lengths $13$ and $14$.

The frontier is not tied to this architecture, and it is not at
thirteen. The classification of \cref{sec:germs} below left a few germs of
complexity $16$ undecided. For one of them, Kissat completes the germ to a
rule for the lengths $2,\dots,13$ in $442$\,s (file
\texttt{delta13\_chaotic.txt}); fixing the same germ and asking for the
lengths $2,\dots,14$, together with the links and the pumping constraints
of \cref{sec:encoding}, it finds in $1758$\,s a rule for all lengths up to
fourteen, with a half-line of an entirely different kind
(\cref{fig:halflines}).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chaotic}
The five-state rule $\delta_{14}$ of \cref{tab:delta14} synchronizes the
line of length $n$ at time $2n-2$ for every $2\le n\le14$ and fails for
$n=15$ (a cell fires at time $22$). Its half-line diagram uses $16$
neighbourhoods besides $(\mathsf L,\mathsf L,\mathsf L)$ on all cells with
$t+i\le10^4$, has no firing cell there, and satisfies $\mathrm{PUMP}(n,n')$
for all $4\le n<n'\le800$; for $n\le5000$, $s\le3$ and $L\le24$ its input
anti-diagonals have no stretch to which \cref{lem:left} applies.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct simulation with the programs \texttt{fsspcheck.c},
\texttt{germcompare.py}, \texttt{germext.c} and \texttt{pumping.py}.
\end{proof}

The half-line of $\delta_{14}$ (\cref{fig:halflines}(b)) is chaotic. For
$3\le t<1500$, cell~$1$ takes only the values
$\mathsf L,\mathsf G$, cell~$2$ only $\mathsf L,\mathsf A$, and all other
cells only $\mathsf L,\mathsf B$; on the latter the germ is the elementary
cellular automaton \emph{rule $22$} in the numbering of \cite{Wolfram83}
(a cell becomes $\mathsf B$ if and only if exactly one cell of its
neighbourhood is $\mathsf B$), a classical example of chaotic behaviour, driven at its left end by the two border
columns. The reflected triangles of $\delta_{14}$ end with a recognisable
wave: for $4\le n\le14$ the configuration one step before firing is
$\mathsf L\,\mathsf G^{\,n-2}\,\mathsf B$, and the state $\mathsf G$, which
the half-line uses only in its first column, marks the firing wave against
the chaotic background. This mechanism does not extend: with the germ of
$\delta_{14}$ fixed, no rule synchronizes all lengths $2,\dots,15$ with
these configurations before firing (Kissat, unsatisfiable after about
$30$ minutes); without this restriction the lengths $2,\dots,15$ remain
undecided after several hours. The half-line has non-periodic structure
everywhere, so it passes both barriers of \cref{sec:pumping} trivially, and the only arguments that apply to it are
completion tests for longer lines, which are expensive. Consequently, the
necessary conditions used in this paper, restricted to lengths at most $14$
(together with the pumping constraints up to $40$ and the half-line up to
anti-diagonal $78$), are satisfiable, and a refutation of five-state
minimal-time solutions along these lines must involve lines of length at
least $15$.

A second rule for all lengths up to fourteen, $\delta_{14}^{\mathrm b}$
(file \texttt{delta14b.txt}; it fails at $n=15$), was found in $29$\,s on
another germ of complexity $16$. Its half-line is of the periodic kind: a
boundary layer of four cells followed by a region that is periodic in time
and space. With a boundary layer of four cells the bound of
\cref{lem:left} (with $s=5$) lies far beyond the lengths we can simulate,
but the half-line is eventually regular: the certificate described after
\cref{cor:regular} succeeds with $t_1=600$ and $P=12$ (program
\texttt{germpersist.py}). Hence, by \cref{cor:regular}, no completion of
this germ is a minimal-time solution either. The same certificate, with
$P=6$, re-proves \cref{cor:germ12} without any length bound.

\subsection{Half-lines of low complexity}\label{sec:germs}

For a rule $\delta$ and a bound $M$, the \emph{germ} of $\delta$ below $M$
is the restriction of $\delta$ to the neighbourhoods
$(C_\infty(t-1,i-1),C_\infty(t-1,i),C_\infty(t-1,i+1))$ of the cells
$(t,i)$ with $t\ge1$ and $t+i\le M$ of its half-line diagram, and its
\emph{complexity} $c_M(\delta)$ is the number of these neighbourhoods other
than $(\mathsf L,\mathsf L,\mathsf L)$. The germ determines $C_\infty$ on
all cells with $t+i\le M$. The rules of \cref{thm:frontier} have $c_{78}=22$
and Mazoyer's rule has $c_{78}=57$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:germs}
Every five-state minimal-time solution $\delta$ satisfies $c_{78}(\delta)\ge15$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof (computer-assisted)]
Let $\delta$ be a five-state minimal-time solution with $c_{78}(\delta)\le14$.
Order the cells of $C_\infty$ by anti-diagonal and, within an anti-diagonal,
by increasing time; after exchanging the names $\mathsf A$ and $\mathsf B$
if necessary, $\mathsf A$ occurs before $\mathsf B$ in this order (if at
all). By \eqref{eq:neverfire}, \cref{lem:return,lem:pumping}, the germ of
$\delta$ below $78$ produces no firing cell, never produces $\mathsf L$ at
the front, and satisfies $\mathrm{PUMP}(n,n')$ for $4\le n<n'\le40$. The
program \texttt{germdfs.c} enumerates all germs with these properties, with
at most $14$ neighbourhoods besides $(\mathsf L,\mathsf L,\mathsf L)$ and
with $\mathsf A$ before $\mathsf B$, by a depth-first search over the cells
in the above order in which the value of a neighbourhood is chosen when it
first occurs: $1.0\cdot10^8$ nodes, $244$ germs. For $230$ of them the
formula $\mathrm{MT}_5(\{2,\dots,10\};78,39)$ (without symmetry breaking,
which the enumeration has already performed) together with the germ is
unsatisfiable; a single formula in which a selector variable chooses the
germ is refuted by CaDiCaL in $4$\,s, with an LRAT proof ($11$\,MB) checked
by \texttt{lrat-check} in under a second. Each of the remaining $14$ germs
determines $C_\infty$ far beyond anti-diagonal $78$ (no new neighbourhood
occurs up to anti-diagonal $3300$), and on these extended diagrams
\cref{lem:left} applies with $s=1$: for each germ there are $n\le163$ and
$L\le5$ such that the two input anti-diagonals of the line of length $n$ are
$L$-periodic on $[1,Y]$ with $Y-1>16L$ (for instance $n=28$, $L=1$, $Y=19$).
Hence no rule containing one of the $244$ germs is a minimal-time solution.
\end{proof}

The same pipeline, run on the $9{,}787$ germs of complexity at most $16$
($1.5\cdot10^9$ nodes), shows how the two kinds of arguments share the
work (\cref{tab:germs}). The completion test for the lengths $2,\dots,10$
refutes $8{,}824$ germs (one LRAT proof of $2.0$\,GB, found in $556$\,s and
checked in $51$\,s). Of the $963$ survivors, $931$ are refuted by
\cref{lem:left} or \cref{lem:pumping} applied to their half-lines, each of
which is determined by the germ far enough for the lemma to apply (we
simulate up to anti-diagonal $10^4$, and for two germs the input
anti-diagonals of the line of length $20{,}000$). Used alone, the half-line arguments
refute $8{,}263$ of the $9{,}787$ germs, without any SAT call. Among the
$32$ germs that pass both filters (one of which is not determined beyond
anti-diagonal $94$) are germs with chaotic or self-similar half-lines, on
which \cref{lem:left} has no grip, and germs whose periodic regions start
too far from the border for the bound of the lemma to be reached within our
simulation range. The completion test for the lengths $2,\dots,13$ refutes
$8$ of them (one LRAT proof of $1.4$\,GB, found in $318$\,s and checked in
$33$\,s); $23$ of the remaining $24$ do have completions for these
lengths (the last one is undecided after $900$\,s). Of these $24$, $8$ have
eventually regular half-lines (certified with periods $P\le48$) and are
excluded by \cref{cor:regular}, although $7$ of them admit completions for
all lengths up to $13$. For the last $16$ germs, among them the germ of $\delta_{14}$,
the certificate fails (periods $P\le48$, block periods at most $24$,
starting times up to $1500$); the half-lines we inspected are chaotic, and
none of our half-line arguments applies to them. Their completion problems
become hard at length $14$. Two of the $16$ have complexity exactly $15$,
and their half-lines coincide on all cells with $t\ge1$ (checked for
$t\le1500$) after the renaming $\mathsf G\mapsto\mathsf A$,
$\mathsf A\mapsto\mathsf B$ of the states of the first one: the first
reuses the state $\mathsf G$ of the general on its half-line and never uses
$\mathsf B$ there, the second never uses $\mathsf G$ after time $0$. A
counterexample-guided search completes both of them to rules for all lengths
up to $14$: $\delta_{14}^{\mathrm c}$ (file \texttt{delta14c.txt}) and
$\delta_{14}^{\mathrm d}$ (file \texttt{delta14d.txt}, found in $376$\,s);
both fail at $n=15$ (for $\delta_{14}^{\mathrm d}$ a cell fires at time
$22$), and both end with a uniform configuration before firing,
$\mathsf G^n$ and $\mathsf A^n$ respectively, for $4\le n\le14$. Raising
the bound of \cref{prop:germs} to $16$ therefore amounts to refuting this
one half-line in its two labellings, and a completion test can only do so
with lines of length at least $15$. For most of the other survivors the
lengths $2,\dots,14$ are undecided after $40$ minutes.

The same filters, applied in a different order, reduce the germs of
complexity exactly $17$ to a few hundred. The enumeration with bound $17$
visits $6.1\cdot10^{9}$ nodes and returns $53{,}081$ germs; those of
complexity at most $16$ are exactly the $9{,}787$ germs of
\cref{tab:germs}, and $43{,}294$ have complexity $17$. On the half-lines
extended to anti-diagonal $1300$, \cref{lem:left} refutes $34{,}613$ of them
and \cref{lem:pumping} (lengths up to $650$) another $637$. The completion
test for the lengths $2,\dots,10$ refutes $6{,}834$ of the remaining
$8{,}044$ (one LRAT proof of $1.9$\,GB, found in $440$\,s and checked in
$33$\,s). Extending the half-lines of the $1{,}210$ survivors to
anti-diagonals $3300$ and $10^4$, the two lemmas refute $903$ more, and the
regularity certificate excludes $120$ of the rest by \cref{cor:regular}
(periods $P\le48$, block periods at most $24$). This leaves $187$ germs of
complexity $17$. We ran the completion test for the lengths $2,\dots,13$
on $30$ of them only: $11$ are refuted, $6$ are undecided within $10^6$
conflicts, and $13$ have completions. The first of these extends to a fifth
rule for all lengths up to $14$, $\delta_{14}^{\mathrm e}$ (file
\texttt{delta14e.txt}; its half-line is irregular), which ends with a
uniform configuration $\mathsf A^n$ and fails at $n=15$ by a single step:
a cell fires at time $27$ instead of $28$.

\subsection{Where the difficulty lies}

\paragraph{The half-line determines everything} Take any of the rules found
above, fix the transitions it uses on the half-line up to anti-diagonal $78$
(between $39$ and $50$ transitions, counting $(\mathsf L,\mathsf L,\mathsf L)$)
and ask whether the remaining transitions
can be completed so that all lengths $2,\dots,N$ synchronize. For the
five-state germs of \cref{tab:five}, Kissat decides this in less than one second: for
the rule of \cref{tab:five} with lengths $2,\dots,10$ the completion exists
for $N=8$ and not for $N=12$; the other germs fail already for $N=8$.
The chaotic germ of \cref{prop:chaotic} is an exception: its completions
for $N=12$, $13$ and $14$ take $2$\,s, $442$\,s and $1758$\,s (the last
one with links).
As a sanity check on a genuine solution, fixing the $58$ transitions that
Mazoyer's rule uses on its half-line up to anti-diagonal $78$ (that is,
$c_{78}=57$ and $(\mathsf L,\mathsf L,\mathsf L)$) leaves a
completion problem for the lengths $3,\dots,12$ that Kissat solves in $4$\,s.
Conversely, the unsatisfiable core returned for such a germ (the rule of
\cref{tab:five} with lengths $2,\dots,10$, completion required for
$2,\dots,12$) contains $38$ of its $39$ transitions, and shrinking it
requires solving exactly the hard subproblems; nogoods learned from the
completion problem, as in a logic-based Benders decomposition, would
therefore be almost as specific as the germs themselves.

\paragraph{Size of the search} We estimated the size of the natural
look-ahead search tree---half-line transitions fixed in order of first use,
anti-diagonal by anti-diagonal, a node being discarded when the full formula
with the partial table as assumptions is refuted within $3000$ conflicts---
with Knuth's random-probe estimator \cite{Knuth75}. For four states ($\mathrm{MT}_4$ with
lengths $2,\dots,9$) the mean over $30$ probes is $27$ nodes. For five
states ($\mathrm{MT}_5(\{2,\dots,12\};78,39)$ with links) three probes gave
$4.2\cdot10^{11}$, $3.1\cdot10^{17}$ and $8.3\cdot10^{18}$; adding the
lengths up to $18$ prunes more strongly along some paths but not in general
(three probes: $2.2\cdot10^{5}$, $4.2\cdot10^{12}$, $1.0\cdot10^{15}$). The estimator has
a large variance, but the gap of at least ten orders of magnitude is robust
and explains why our runs on the unrestricted five-state formulas with the
pumping constraints for $N=12$, $14$ and $18$ (up to about $1.5$ CPU-hours
each) did not terminate. Clause
learning compresses such trees considerably, and cube-and-conquer
\cite{HeuleEtAl11} parallelizes them almost linearly, but a complete
five-state search along these lines is a computation of a much larger scale
than the ones reported here.
