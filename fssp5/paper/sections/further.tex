\section{Further experiments}\label{app:further}

This appendix records the experiments summarised in
\cref{sec:five}. Every rule was checked with the brute-force checker
\texttt{fsspcheck.c} and with an independent simulation. Unsatisfiability
claims called \emph{certified} come with LRAT proofs checked by
\texttt{lrat-check}; all other solver results are reported as the solvers
returned them.

\subsection{Four more rules for all lengths up to fourteen}\label{app:more14}

A second rule for all lengths up to fourteen, $\delta_{14}^{\mathrm b}$
(file \texttt{delta14b.txt}), was found in $29$\,s on another germ of
complexity $16$. Its half-line is of the periodic kind: a boundary layer
of four cells followed by a region that is periodic in time and space. With
a boundary layer of four cells the bound of \cref{lem:left} (with $s=5$)
lies far beyond the lengths we can simulate, but the half-line is
eventually regular: the certificate described after \cref{cor:regular}
succeeds with $t_1=600$ and $P=12$ (program \texttt{germpersist.py}). Hence,
by \cref{cor:regular}, no completion of this germ is a minimal-time
solution.

Two of the $16$ germs that survive the classification of \cref{sec:germs}
have complexity exactly $15$, and their half-lines coincide on all cells
with $t\ge1$ (checked for $t\le1500$) after the renaming
$\mathsf G\mapsto\mathsf A$, $\mathsf A\mapsto\mathsf B$ of the states of
the first one: the first reuses the state $\mathsf G$ of the general on
its half-line and never uses $\mathsf B$ there, the second never uses
$\mathsf G$ after time $0$. A counterexample-guided search completes both
of them to rules for all lengths up to $14$: $\delta_{14}^{\mathrm c}$ (file
\texttt{delta14c.txt}) and $\delta_{14}^{\mathrm d}$ (file
\texttt{delta14d.txt}, found in $376$\,s). Raising the bound of
\cref{prop:germs} to $16$ therefore amounts to refuting this one half-line
in its two labellings, and a completion test can only do so with lines of
length at least $15$ (\cref{app:cube}).

Among the germs of complexity $17$ (\cref{app:k17}), the first one that
passes the completion test for the lengths $2,\dots,13$ extends to a fifth
rule for all lengths up to $14$, $\delta_{14}^{\mathrm e}$ (file
\texttt{delta14e.txt}, found in $401$\,s); its half-line is irregular.

\Cref{tab:more14} compares the five rules. All of them end with a
configuration before firing that is uniform, or uniform except at the end
cells. All five fail at $n=15$ in the same way: the first cells to fire
are in the right half of the line, too early by one to seven steps. For
$\delta_{14}$ the mechanism behind the configurations
$\mathsf L\,\mathsf G^{n-2}\,\mathsf B$ provably does not extend: with its
germ fixed, no rule synchronizes all lengths $2,\dots,15$ with these
configurations before firing (Kissat, unsatisfiable after about $30$
minutes). The corresponding tests for the uniform configurations of
$\delta_{14}^{\mathrm c}$ and $\delta_{14}^{\mathrm d}$ were stopped without
an answer after about $2.5$ hours each.

\subsection{Germs of complexity at most sixteen}\label{app:k16}

The pipeline of \cref{prop:germs}, run on the $9{,}787$ germs of
complexity at most $16$ ($1.5\cdot10^9$ nodes), gives the numbers of
\cref{tab:germs}. The completion test for the lengths $2,\dots,10$ refutes
$8{,}824$ germs (one LRAT proof of $2.0$\,GB, found in $556$\,s and checked
in $51$\,s). Of the $963$ survivors, $931$ are refuted by \cref{lem:left}
or \cref{lem:pumping} applied to their half-lines, each of which is
determined by the germ far enough for the lemma to apply (we simulate up to
anti-diagonal $10^4$, and for two germs the input anti-diagonals of the
line of length $20{,}000$). Used alone, the half-line arguments refute
$8{,}263$ of the $9{,}787$ germs, without any SAT call. Among the $32$ germs
that pass both filters (one of which is not determined beyond anti-diagonal
$94$) are germs with chaotic or self-similar half-lines, on which
\cref{lem:left} has no grip, and germs whose periodic regions start too far
from the border for the bound of the lemma to be reached within our
simulation range. The completion test for the lengths $2,\dots,13$ refutes
$8$ of them (one LRAT proof of $1.4$\,GB, found in $318$\,s and checked in
$33$\,s); $23$ of the remaining $24$ do have completions for these lengths
(the last one is undecided after $900$\,s). Of these $24$, $8$ have
eventually regular half-lines (certified with periods $P\le48$) and are
excluded by \cref{cor:regular}, although $7$ of them admit completions for
all lengths up to $13$. For the last $16$ germs, among them the germ of
$\delta_{14}$, the certificate fails (periods $P\le48$, block periods at
most $24$, starting times up to $1500$); the half-lines we inspected are
chaotic, and none of our half-line arguments applies to them. Their
completion problems become hard at length $14$: for most of them the lengths
$2,\dots,14$ are undecided after $40$ minutes.

\subsection{Germs of complexity seventeen}\label{app:k17}

The enumeration with bound $17$ visits $6.1\cdot10^{9}$ nodes and returns
$53{,}081$ germs; those of complexity at most $16$ are exactly the
$9{,}787$ germs of \cref{tab:germs}, and $43{,}294$ have complexity $17$.
Here we applied the filters in a different order. On the half-lines
extended to anti-diagonal $1300$, \cref{lem:left} refutes $34{,}613$ of
them and \cref{lem:pumping} (lengths up to $650$) another $637$. The
completion test for the lengths $2,\dots,10$ refutes $6{,}834$ of the
remaining $8{,}044$ (one LRAT proof of $1.9$\,GB, found in $440$\,s and
checked in $33$\,s). Extending the half-lines of the $1{,}210$ survivors to
anti-diagonals $3300$ and $10^4$, the two lemmas refute $903$ more, and the
regularity certificate excludes $120$ of the rest by \cref{cor:regular}
(periods $P\le48$, block periods at most $24$). This leaves $187$ germs of
complexity $17$. We ran the completion test for the lengths $2,\dots,13$ on
$30$ of them only: $11$ are refuted, $6$ are undecided within $10^6$
conflicts, and $13$ have completions, one of which yields
$\delta_{14}^{\mathrm e}$.

\subsection{Lines of length fifteen for the complexity-15 half-line}\label{app:cube}

We split the completion problems of the two labellings of the
complexity-$15$ half-line (\cref{app:more14}) for the lengths
$2,\dots,15$ into $2{,}731$ (first labelling) and $3{,}070$ (second
labelling) cubes with the look-ahead solver
\textsf{march\_cu} (depth $12$) \cite{HeuleEtAl11} and solved the cubes
with CaDiCaL under assumptions and, for the hard ones, with Kissat on the
formula extended by the cube, which was much faster on them. After about
ten CPU-hours, $1{,}898$ and $1{,}376$ cubes are refuted and none is
satisfiable, but $14$ and $375$ cubes resisted the limits we set (up to
$2\cdot10^{6}$ conflicts, or ten minutes of Kissat) and $819$ and $1{,}319$
were not attempted: both problems remain undecided. Single runs on the
whole problems were not more successful: Kissat on the second labelling
with the lengths $2,\dots,15$ stopped without an answer after two hours,
and on the first labelling with the lengths $2,\dots,14$ and the
non-firing constraints of the partial triangles of the lengths
$15,\dots,26$ up to relative time $12$ after three hours.

\subsection{Unsatisfiable cores}\label{app:cores}

For the periodic germs, the completion problem is decided in less than a
second, but its unsatisfiable cores are large: for the germ of the rule of
\cref{tab:five} with lengths $2,\dots,10$, completion required for the
lengths $2,\dots,12$, the core contains $38$ of its $39$ transitions, and
shrinking it requires solving exactly the hard subproblems. Nogoods learned
from the completion problem, as in a logic-based Benders decomposition,
would therefore be almost as specific as the germs themselves.
